1. What makes this different?

I'm tired of auditioning for a job I already know how to do

Somewhere along the way, being good at your job stopped being enough. Now you're apparently supposed to be a thought leader about it too

The problem isn't jobs. It's the performance

LinkedIn is genuinely useful. It connects people, makes careers visible and helps employers find talent. I just hate what it asks me to do to stay visible

Celebrate every minor milestone. Post a humblebrag about your team's incredible journey. Tell strangers you're thrilled to announce something. Comment on a CEO's inspirational nonsense so the algorithm remembers you exist

And if you don't play along? You start wondering whether being quietly competent is enough

Job peacocking is exhausting

I don't want to build a personal brand. I want to do good work, get paid fairly and occasionally find out whether there's a better job somewhere else

And when I look at a job, I don't need another paragraph about a fast-paced environment, rockstar colleagues or a company that's basically one big family

I want to know what it's actually like on a Tuesday afternoon

So here's the experiment

What if we kept the useful part of professional networking and got rid of the theatre?

Share a real job opening. No pitch attached. Someone asks a question. People who know something can answer. The person who posted the link doesn't get to launch into a sales speech unless someone actually asks

That's it. No feed of career wisdom. No personal-brand Olympics. No obligation to announce that you're humbled and honoured

How it works

1. Post the job, not the pitch
Share a link to a real opening

2. Questions first, answers second
Ask about the culture, the work or the stuff the ad conveniently forgot

3. No personal brand required
A pseudonym is enough. You don't need a CV or a professional origin story

This is an experiment, not a promise that every answer is true or every company is terrible. Ask questions, share what you know and use your judgement
